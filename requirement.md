# **CHOOP — Coupon Meets Connection**

Website Design Transformation · Version 1.0 · Based on the captured fitness website

We're going to reuse the **complete design system and section-by-section structure** of the fitness website, but transform its purpose, content, imagery, and user experience for **CHOOP**, a coupon-based social discovery platform.

The principle is simple:

**Preserve the visual DNA. Change the entire experience.**

# **choop**

Discover. Share. Save.

Concept identity

Original website

Fitness → Training → Transformation → Membership

CHOOP adaptation

Discover → Claim Coupons → Connect → Redeem → Share

I'm using the earlier **coupon + social networking** idea as CHOOP's foundation. That means coupons are not just discounts; they can become a reason for people to discover places, share offers, and enjoy experiences together.

This is a proposed product direction, not a claim that every feature has already been finalized.

## **4. Additional CHOOP-specific functionality**

The original landing page is mostly promotional. A coupon platform also needs a functional product layer. I recommend separating this into reusable modules:

- **Coupon discovery:** Search, categories, location filters, offer sorting, and merchant detail pages.
- **Coupon management:** Claim, save, expiry, terms, redemption state, and redemption history.
- **Social features:** Profiles, friend sharing, optional group experiences, and shared offers.
- **Merchant features:** Business onboarding, offer creation, coupon limits, and redemption validation.
- **Trust and prevention:** Unique redemption records, server-side validation, expiry rules, and safeguards against duplicate use.

For the public landing page, we don't need to expose every feature. The experience should focus on discovery, excitement, and joining CHOOP.

## **6. Design rules for the next iteration**

1. **Maintain the original website's overall visual composition**, not just its colors.
2. Keep the same sequence of sections, white space, photography proportions, card geometry, and typographic treatment.
3. Adapt every visible image and piece of copy to CHOOP's purpose.
4. Introduce coupon-specific UI patterns without making the homepage look like a crowded e-commerce marketplace.
5. Keep merchant and admin functionality separate from the consumer-facing marketing page.
6. Design mobile-first behavior for discovering and redeeming coupons in physical locations.
